# Gates core/source record

## Rule identity and classification

- Style ID: `gates`; Bill Gates-inspired analytical style.
- Mandatory rule ID: `gates.evidence_relation`.
- Classification: core.
- Evidence class: project synthesis based on first-person analytical orientation
  and limited official transcript observations, not an official “Gates method”.

## Required propositions and applicability

1. Analytical guidance relates evidence, quantities or system elements to a stated
   comparison or system point.
2. When labels, units, assumptions or qualifications are needed to interpret supplied
   evidence, preserve those relationships and qualifiers.

The alternatives need not all occur at once. State what the evidence supports or
how comparison/system elements relate; hierarchy and relevant annotations aid that
interpretation. Qualifiers are conditional on interpretive need: do not invent
assumptions just to fill a template. Preserve supplied scope and measurement meaning;
a label, unit or test restriction cannot be removed merely for whitespace or brevity.
Whitespace is compatible when relationships remain; high density is not necessary.

analytical speaker concerned with numbers and systems. This supports analytical

**G3 — Official transcript observations.** The 2009 transcript connects historical
quantities, comparisons and interacting interventions with stated problems; it also
includes humor and demonstration. These observations motivate analytical relationships,
not universal layout metrics or a ban on other expression forms.
Source: [TED, Gates's 2009 talk transcript](https://blog.ted.com/bill_gates_talk/).
Reading scope: public transcript text, rechecked 2026-10-04; no complete video viewing,

The complete relationship and necessary-label/unit/assumption safeguards are the
project's practical synthesis; neither source is claimed to state this rule verbatim.
No formal creator-method, one-page complete-argument, mandatory-density or fixed
numerical presentation formula is established by this reading. No general comparative
effectiveness claim is made. Multi-speaker event text must be attributed to its
actual speaker, not automatically Gates because his name is in an event title.

## Boundary examples

**Compatible:** A sparse A/B average-response-time comparison keeps its metric,
units, shared test assumptions and applicability limit, with evidence explicitly
supporting that comparison. A system overview similarly retains labeled elements
and meaningful connections supporting its stated system point.

**Conflicting:** Delete units/assumptions needed to interpret the comparison, or use
large disconnected quantities as decoration. Higher density, a formal theme or more
charts cannot repair the lost evidence relationship.

Examples judge guidance meaning, not generated diagrams or actual benchmark results.
